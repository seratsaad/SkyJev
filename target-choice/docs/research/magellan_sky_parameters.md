# Magellan / Las Campanas parameters for the observing-night simulator and ETC

Compiled 2026-09-24 for `obsassist`. Machine-readable twin: [`magellan_sky_parameters.json`](magellan_sky_parameters.json) (every leaf = `{value, unit, source, confidence, note}`). The tables below are generated from that JSON, so the two files always agree.

**Confidence key:** `doc` = stated in the cited source. `approx` = read off a plot, derived by arithmetic from documented numbers, or taken from a nearby proxy site. `**EST**` = not found in any public source; the value is my reasoned estimate (the note says why).

## 0. Found vs. estimated (read this first)

- **Well documented:** collecting area (LCO ETC uses 30.638 m²), extinction ugriBV/YJH at LCO, DIMM seeing percentiles, Magellan delivered IQ, wind limit (15.6 m/s), clear-night fractions, humidity/PWV climatology, all four instruments' detectors, scales, gains, read noise, **readout times**, dispersers and resolving powers, the catalog format and GUIs, and the KS91, Patat 2006, ESO-ETC and Tokovinin model equations.
- **Derived (approx):** per-wavelength total efficiency curves, which I computed from the official LCO ETC zero-point files (github.com/gblancm/lcoetc). S/N anchors come from an offline re-run of that ETC's public code; I did not submit the web form. Also derived: FIRE throughput and read noise read off plots, UBVRI dark sky (CTIO proxy, which matches the measured LCO V = 21.95), and the colors of scattered moonlight.
- **Estimated (not published):** telescope slew rates, settle time, elevation limit; Magellan humidity/dew-point closure limit and reopening rule (ESO La Silla proxy used); the science twilight convention; IMACS full well and filter-change time; LDSS3 mask/longslit acquisition and wheel-move times; FIRE/MIKE acquisition overheads; extinction in U, R, I, z, Ks; seeing correlation time at LCO specifically.
- **Not documented publicly:** a scriptable/remote TCS command API. Only instrument-side helpers are documented: FIRE IDL `fatv_*`, and the IMACS/LDSS3 alignment tools that compute offsets for the TO.
- **Source conflicts to be aware of.** FIRE Fowler-1 read noise is 10 e⁻ in the current LCO manual but 16–22 e⁻ in PASP 2013. The FIRE echelle scale is 0.15″/pix in PASP but 0.18″/pix on the spec pages. MIKE peak throughput is 37%/20% slit-to-detector in the manual but 17%/13% end-to-end in the 2022 handout. LDSS3 VPH-All peak efficiency is 34% in the manual but ≈20% from the ETC zero points.

## 1. Model equations (for implementation)

### 1a. Krisciunas & Schaefer (1991) moonlit sky, V band
Source: [KS91 ADS scan](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1991PASP..103.1033K) (doi:10.1086/132921). I read the equations directly from the scan and cross-checked them against the [DESI specsim implementation](https://github.com/desihub/specsim/blob/main/specsim/atmosphere.py).

```
B[nL]  = 34.08 * exp(20.7233 - 0.92104 * V)          # (1) V in mag/arcsec^2  <->  nanoLamberts
V      = (20.7233 - ln(B/34.08)) / 0.92104
B0(Z)  = Bzen * 10^(-0.4 k (X(Z) - 1)) * X(Z)         # (2) dark sky vs zenith distance Z
X(Z)   = (1 - 0.96 sin^2 Z)^-0.5                       # (3) 'scattering airmass' (use for sky AND Moon in eq 15)
X_ext(Z) = [cos Z + 0.025 exp(-11 cos Z)]^-1           # (14) extinction airmass for directly viewed objects
m_moon = -12.73 + 0.026|a| + 4e-9 a^4                 # (9) a = lunar phase angle in deg (0 = full)
I*     = 10^(-0.4 (m_moon + 16.57))                    # (8) => I* = 10^(-0.4(3.84 + 0.026|a| + 4e-9 a^4))  (20)
f(rho) = 10^5.36 [1.06 + cos^2 rho] + 10^(6.15 - rho/40)   # (21) rho = Moon-target separation, deg, rho >= 10
         (Rayleigh C_R = 2.27e5 ~ 10^5.36;  for rho < 10 deg use f_M = 6.2e7 rho^-2   (19))
B_moon = f(rho) * I* * 10^(-0.4 k X(Zm)) * [1 - 10^(-0.4 k X(Z))]   # (15) nL
dV     = -2.5 log10[(B_moon + B0(Z)) / B0(Z)]          # (22)
```
- Opposition surge: for |α| < 7°, multiply B_moon by 1.00–1.35. It is "about 35% brighter" exactly at full Moon, and does not apply during an eclipse. The Moon's distance changes m_moon by up to ±0.12 mag.
- KS91 calibration: k = 0.172, V_zen = 21.587 (B_zen = 79.0 nL). Quoted rms accuracy is 23% (8–11% against daytime data). **Unit test:** KS91 Table 2 is stored in the JSON under `sky_models.KS91_moonlight.table2_test_values_nL`. It assumes Moon ZA = 60° and k = 0.172, with ρ measured along the great circle through the Moon and the zenith, so ρ = 60° is the zenith. For example α = 60°, ρ = 90° gives 204 nL.
- For LCO use k_V = 0.144 ([Krisciunas+2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.05146)) and B_zen from V_zen ≈ 21.8–21.95.

### 1b. Scaling V-band moonlight to other bands
1. **Physical method** ([Noll+2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2003) §3.1, used by ESO SkyCalc). Split scattering into Rayleigh and Mie terms, each with its own transmission curve. The Moon's spectrum is the solar spectrum scaled to the KS91 V brightness:
   `B_R/M(λ) = f_R/M(ρ) I*(λ) t(λ)^X_moon (1 − t_R/M(λ)^X0)`, where `f_R = 10^5.70 (1.06+cos²ρ)` and `f_M = 10^(7.15−ρ/40)`. These are KS91 × 2.2 and × 10, tuned for Paranal. `t_R`, `t_M` are the Rayleigh (∝λ⁻⁴) and aerosol parts of the extinction curve. Phase angle α ≈ arccos(1 − 2·FLI).
2. **Simple color method.** Add a per-band color to the KS91 V moonlight: m_moon,band = m_moon,V + C_band. The SkyCalc full-Moon excess colors, derived from the LCO ETC's own sky files, are U−V −0.18, B−V +0.17, R−V +0.02, I−V +0.14, Z−V +0.22, J−V +0.62, H−V +0.70 (Vega; each value is m_band − m_V of the moonlight). Moonlight is therefore much bluer than sunlight (solar B−V = 0.65).
3. **Sanity table.** The Walker (1987) CTIO sky versus days from new Moon is in the JSON under `sky_models.moonlight_band_scaling.Walker_table`. It gives typical values that don't depend on geometry.

### 1c. Twilight sky brightness (Patat, Ugolnikov & Postylyakov 2006, Paranal)
m(ζ) = a0 + a1(ζ−95) + a2(ζ−95)², where ζ = 90° − h_sun. The fit is valid for 95° ≤ ζ ≤ 105°, at the zenith (|α| ≤ 40°), with the Moon down. Source: [arXiv:astro-ph/0604128](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0604128), Table 1.

| Band | a0 | a1 | a2 | rms | γ (95–100°) | h=−6° | h=−9° | h=−12° | h=−15° | h=−18° (extrap.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U | 11.78 | 1.376 | -0.039 | 0.24 | 1.23 | 13.12 | 16.66 | 19.5 | 21.64 | 23.08 |
| B | 11.84 | 1.411 | -0.041 | 0.12 | 1.24 | 13.21 | 16.83 | 19.71 | 21.85 | 23.25 |
| V | 11.84 | 1.518 | -0.057 | 0.18 | 1.14 | 13.3 | 17.0 | 19.67 | 21.32 | 21.94 |
| R | 11.4 | 1.567 | -0.064 | 0.29 | 1.09 | 12.9 | 16.64 | 19.23 | 20.67 | 20.95 |
| I | 10.93 | 1.47 | -0.062 | 0.4 | 0.94 | 12.34 | 15.82 | 18.18 | 19.43 | 19.56 |

- The sky reaches the night level at ζ ≈ 105–106°. γ × dζ/dt (≈ 0.23°/min near the equinox at Paranal) gives a decline of 0.22 (I) to 0.28 (U) mag arcsec⁻² per minute during nautical twilight.
- **Implementation:** convert the fit to a twilight excess flux, F_tw = F_fit(ζ) − F_dark,Paranal (Paranal dark values: 22.28/22.64/21.61/20.87/19.71). Clip it at ≥ 0 and add it to the LCO dark sky. Set F_tw = 0 for ζ > 105°. The −18° column is an extrapolation and must not be used directly.

### 1d. Seeing versus airmass and wavelength, and the outer scale
From the ESO ETC ([helpfors](https://www.eso.org/observing/etc/doc/helpfors.html)):
```
FWHM_IQ  = sqrt(FWHM_atm^2 + FWHM_tel^2 + FWHM_ins^2)
FWHM_atm = s * X^0.6 * (lam/500nm)^-0.2 * sqrt(1 + F_Kolb * 2.183 * (r0/L0)^0.356)     # 0 if sqrt arg < 0
r0       = 0.100 m * s^-1 * (lam/500nm)^1.2 * X^-0.6          (s = DIMM seeing at 500 nm, zenith, arcsec)
F_Kolb   = 1/(1 + 300 D/L0) - 1        # VLT (D=8.2, L0=46): -0.981644;  Magellan (D=6.5, L0=25): -0.9873
FWHM_tel = 0.000212 * lam[nm] / D[m]  arcsec
Tokovinin (2002): (FWHM_vK / FWHM_Kolmogorov)^2 = 1 - 2.183 (r0/L0)^0.356,  valid L0/r0 > 20 (+-1%)
```
- Use L0 = 25 m at LCO ([Floyd+2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.0174)). Check: 0.63″ DIMM seeing gives FWHM_atm ≈ 0.51″ at 500 nm and ≈ 0.27″ at 2.15 µm.
- Magellan optics add 0.17″ at the Nasmyth ports used by LDSS3/MagIC and 0.30″ at IMACS, in quadrature. The empirical Magellan relation is FWHM = 0.53·DIMM + 0.24″.

### 1e. Seeing within a night (recommended generator, **estimate**)
Model ln(seeing) = μ + night offset + an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, with these parameters:
- μ = ln 0.63 (LCO DIMM median).
- σ_night = 0.25 (night-to-night).
- σ_OU = 0.22 (within night), relaxation time τ ≈ 60 min (plausible range 20–120).
- These give a total σ_ln ≈ 0.33, which matches the LCO quartiles (σ_ln ≈ 0.34).
- The expected 1-h change is ≈ 0.16″, consistent with the VLT persistence-forecast RMSE of 0.17″ and the San Pedro Mártir within-night σ of 0.19″. Racine (1996) measured an e-folding time of 17 min (free atmosphere, Mauna Kea).
- Optional: add a power-law (f^−1.43) component to reproduce sudden jumps (TMT/Herriot+2010).

## 6. How observers operate (documented workflow)
A typical target sequence, as documented:
1. Load the catalog, whose 16-field format includes rotator angle and mode.
2. The TO slews and sets the guide and Shack–Hartmann stars. Active-optics convergence takes "a few minutes".
3. The observer takes a field image (~10 s) with the instrument GUI.
4. The observer runs the alignment tool (`imacs_tomask` / `imacs_slit_align` / LDSS3 mask align / FIRE `fatv`) and tells the TO the offsets.
5. The observer iterates until the offsets are negligible, inserts the disperser, and sets exposure time, number of exposures (Loops), binning and speed.
6. Start the exposure. Stop reads out and saves the elapsed time; Abort discards it.
7. Take arcs and flats as needed (FIRE: an arc at every pointing; MIKE: arcs every 15–60 min).
